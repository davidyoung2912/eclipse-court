# Eclipse Court: Lore Bible (draft 1, decisions locked)

Status: approved direction. No code depends on this yet. Names are accepted for now and may be adjusted later (see section 8).

Design rule for the whole setting: **neither court is good, neither is evil. Both are right about one thing, wrong about another, and both are hiding something.** Whichever side the player controls, the other side must be a believable antagonist, and the player must be able to feel the argument from either seat.

---

## 1. The setting

**Veyra** is a walled realm built around one machine: the **Orrery Court**, a seven-by-seven floor of old stone with five **Beacons** set into it. The Beacons do not light the realm. They *turn* it. Whoever holds them sets the **Hour**, the rippling cycle of light that decides when the realm is bright, when it is dim, and how strong each court is on each square (the game's -2 to +2 luminance cycle).

When the Hour turns freely, crops grow, the dead rest, children are born and the seasons keep their shape. When it is forced or stalled, the realm slides toward the **Eclipse**: a creeping stillness where nothing grows, nothing heals and nothing is allowed to end. (In the game: the Eclipse falls at a set round, and spells fail after it.)

Veyrans call the Court floor, and the ritual contest played on it, simply **the Court**. A match is a *Convening*.

## 2. History

**The Keeper's Age.** For centuries one monarch stood at the centre Beacon and kept the Hour by hand. She was the **Keeper**, and the last Keeper was **Queen Ysmere**. The realm was peaceful because one tired person bore all of it.

**The Stilling.** Ysmere had twins, **Aurel** and **Nocte**. At a dusk in the Queen's thirtieth year on the throne, Nocte lay dying. Ysmere did the one thing a Keeper must never do: she held the Hour still, for one night, to buy time, and then called her daughter back from death with the old craft. Nocte lived. The Hour had stuck. The first Eclipse fell, and a third of Veyra starved before Ysmere, unable to let go of the stalled Hour, gave herself to it and became the thing that holds the Eclipse back: **the Eclipse Queen**. She has not left the Sanctum since.

**The Sundering.** With no Keeper, the Hour began to drift. Aurel and Nocte each took a share of the Beacons and tried to keep the realm turning together. They could not agree on the lesson of the Stilling, and the disagreement hardened into two courts.

**The Convenings.** The first Convening was a peace: a sanctified contest on the Orrery Court, played with sworn champions, so the courts could settle who sets the Hour without burning the realm. It worked for generations. Over time the champions grew to be armies, the stakes grew from a season to a century, and the ritual drifted back into war. Both courts still insist the Convening is law. Neither remembers who lost the last one fairly.

## 3. The two courts

Both courts descend from the Stilling and drew *opposite* conclusions from it.

### The Solari Court: "The Hour must be held."
- **Seat:** the Meridian, a gilded city on the Court's sunward edge.
- **Leader:** the **Solarch** (an elected office, passed on by council, not by blood).
- **Belief:** The Stilling was a failure of vigilance. Ysmere acted alone, in the dark, with no check. So: law, records, daylight, and a Hour that is *watched and fixed* rather than trusted to drift. Night is when things go wrong, and the Solari will not be caught unready again.
- **Virtues:** accountability, fairness, openness, care for the living.
- **What they hide:** The sunwells that keep the Meridian bright are slowly burning the shadows where the Umbral dead rest, and the council knows. They also exile "night-touched" citizens rather than admit how many there are.
- **Craft:** Hold the Light, Mirror (reciprocity and law). Dawn Golems are built of sunlit stone, Lumen Knights take oaths of service.

### The Umbral Court: "The Hour must be paid for."
- **Seat:** the Vesper Halls, a half-buried city on the Court's shaded edge.
- **Leader:** the **Duskmother** (the title passes to whoever the dead will answer to; the first was Nocte).
- **Belief:** The Stilling was theft. Ysmere took a night she had not earned, and the realm paid. So: nothing is free, every gift has a cost, and the Hour must always be allowed to turn, whoever it hurts. The dead are not an end but a *neighbour*, and the Umbral are their keepers.
- **Virtues:** memory, mercy for the dying, loyalty to kin, honesty about cost.
- **What they hide:** The Umbral dead are not all willing. The Duskmother keeps her fallen in service (Revive, Sacrifice) because she cannot bear to let them go, which is exactly the mistake Ysmere made. Gloom Ghouls are mostly volunteers. Not all of them.
- **Craft:** Shroud, Sacrifice. Wraiths tend the dying, Mire Trolls are the Vesper Halls' oldest allies, bound by an ancient debt.

### Why each is right
- The Solari are right that an unwatched Hour breeds disaster.
- The Umbral are right that an Hour held fixed is exactly what caused the Eclipse.

### Why each is wrong
- The Solari's "hold" slides toward a permanent stalled day, the same stilling in a brighter coat.
- The Umbral's "pay the cost" slides toward a court that spends its own people to avoid ever saying "enough".

### Shared craft
Mend, Revive, Turn the Hour, Hasten, Blink, Swap, Conjure, Bind and Exchange come from Ysmere's old Keeper's court. Both courts inherited it. Neither will admit the other's use is legitimate.

## 4. The Eclipse Queen

She is **not a villain** and not an ally. She is the final cost of the Stilling made flesh, and the story's question.

- **What she is:** the stalled Hour with a person inside it. Her boss rules (every piece has extra HP, an early Eclipse, deadly outer squares) are what the realm looks like under her: the world pushed past its limit.
- **What she wants:** to end the war the only way she can. If no one can set the Hour, no one can win; if no one wins, no one is lost. She means it as mercy. It is the Stilling again, forever.
- **What she remembers:** a daughter dying at dusk. She does not regret the choice, only its price.
- **Why she can be fought:** the Eclipse needs a Keeper, and she is failing. Whoever bests her at the Court can take the Hour. That is the trap, and the choice.

### Endgame question: *Who keeps the Hour?*
1. **The Solarch keeps it.** The Hour is fixed and watched. Safe, bright, and eventually still.
2. **The Duskmother keeps it.** The Hour always turns and always costs. Honest, hard, and eventually hollow.
3. **Share it.** The courts alternate. Neither wins; the argument continues. Hardest to earn.
4. **Free the Queen.** Nobody keeps the Hour. It runs itself for the first time in centuries, and nobody knows whether it holds.

No ending is "the good one". Each one is a vote for a way of living, and each costs someone.

## 6. Tone and rules for writing

- **Short, concrete, mythic.** Think old story told by someone who was there, not epic fantasy lore dump.
- **Every character believes they are protecting something.**
- **Same events, two readings.** Any scene we write for one court should be writable from the other's side with a different truth in the foreground.
- **No pure monsters.** Even Ghouls and Golems have a reason to be there.
- **The board is a place, not a menu.** Each board is a named room in the Orrery Court with a past.

## 7. Seeds for characters (for the story-mode step, not yet canon)

- **Wren**, a Sunhawk rider of the Solari, and **Tam**, her brother, a Lumen Knight. Tam falls in the first act at the player's own hand, and in the second act he is a Gloom Ghoul in the Vesper Halls: the player plays him, and has to decide whether he remembers.
- **Ser Halvard Voss**, a Solari commander who truly believes in the Meridian Law and slowly discovers the sunwell secret.
- **Mother Ilse**, a Wraith-keeper of the Umbral who tends the dead and starts to doubt whether the Duskmother still lets them go.
- **A Night Raven scout** who was a Sunhawk rider once and crosses both sides' lines (the person who ties the two stories together).
- **Ysmere** herself, heard before she is seen: letters, fragments, the first Convening's oaths.

## 8. Decisions

1. **Tone:** mythic, as drafted.
2. **Backstory:** the Stilling stays as the engine of the setting.
3. **The Queen:** Ysmere is the twins' parent.
4. **The dead:** necromancy stays. The Umbral secret (the Duskmother keeping her dead in service) is canon.
5. **Endings:** four (Solarch keeps the Hour, Duskmother keeps the Hour, shared, free the Queen).
6. **Names:** Veyra, Ysmere, Aurel, Nocte, the Meridian, the Vesper Halls and the Orrery Court are accepted for now; a light rename pass is allowed before they appear in shipped text.
